The order at the bottom of the chart is four words. Weight bearing as tolerated. Nobody sits down and explains them, so the man reads them himself sometime in the first week and privately decides what they mean, and what he decides is usually not yet.

He is fifty one. Tibia, plated, eleven weeks. The bone is finished. I have the film, there is callus on it, I have put it in front of him and turned it the right way up and pointed at the place where the line stopped being a line.

He will not put the leg down.

He gets from the bed to the chair on the frame with the foot held about two centimetres off the floor, and he does it well. He has had eleven weeks of practice. He has a technique.

And the leg is going. That is the part nobody believes when you say it out loud. The thigh is smaller than the other one and you can see it through the blanket. The knee has started keeping a bend in it even when he is not asking for one. The heel has forgotten what a floor is, so the first time it meets one it will send up a report saying this is wrong, and he will believe the report, because it will be his own leg telling him.

Nothing in a nervous system learns from rest. It has no mechanism for it. It learns from load. You put a small amount of weight through something that is frightened and nothing terrible happens and the system writes that down. Then slightly more. What gets written down is not courage. It is data.

So a man waiting to feel ready is waiting for a signal that is only produced by the thing he is not doing.

I said a version of that to him. Not that well. He listened, he is a polite man, and when I stopped he said something I had no answer for.

He said, I will put it down when I trust it.

Which is the correct sentence. It is the reason the order says as tolerated and not fifty percent by Thursday. There is no way to want it on his behalf. I can show him a film and tell him what the tissue is doing and be right about every part of it and I still cannot supply the tolerance, because tolerance is not a fact about a leg. It is a fact about a person, and it belongs to him.

I went home and I did not sit down.

I have not sat down in a long time. I walk the room when I am talking to somebody. Bed, door, window, bed. I have had years of practice. I have a technique.

Here is the part I did not say to him. I have never let one thing in my life sit still long enough to knit. Anything that hurts, I load it the following morning. I go and find it and put weight through it and call that learning, and I have been calling it learning for a long time, and my results are on file.

Two men. One will not put the leg down and one will not stop walking on it, and from outside those look like opposite conditions. They are the same condition. We have both decided the answer in advance so that neither of us ever has to find out.

His thigh will come back. That is documented, it takes about a year, it is unpleasant and it works. And the day he finally puts it down he will learn that the floor was always going to hold him, and that nothing he did in the eleven weeks made that more or less true.

I am not going to tell him that. He would not believe it from me and he should not.

Tomorrow I will go in and stand at the end of the bed and ask him how much he did today, and he will give me a number, and whatever the number is I am going to say that is good, do that again tomorrow.

That is the whole treatment. There is no second part to it.
