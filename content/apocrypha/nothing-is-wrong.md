---
title: "Nothing Is Wrong"
---

Nothing is wrong. I have to put that at the top because I am going to write things underneath it that make it look like something is wrong and it isn't. Nothing is wrong. I have not slept in three nights.

Sylphie is kind to me. She has never once been unkind to me, not when I was a child and not now. Roxy is kind to me. Eris came back and she is kind to me in her way and she has stayed and stayed and stayed.

Everything I asked for happened. All of it. Every single thing I asked for happened, and I want to know why I am awake.

I fixed the heater on Monday and nobody asked me to. Sylphie said thank you and put her hand on my neck and for about four seconds it was enough. Four seconds. I counted them the second time so I would know I wasn't imagining it. On Wednesday I cleared the ice off the yard before Eris was up and went back to bed with my boots still wet so she would find it and I lay there with my heart going like I was waiting for a verdict, and she hit my shoulder at dinner, and four seconds.

I think this is boredom. I keep telling myself it is boredom because boredom is a thing a man can be ashamed of quietly.

They never ask me for anything. Not one of them. I have three people in this house who love me and they have never needed a single thing from me, so I have to keep inventing things to bring them, and every time I bring one they are grateful and warm and I feel it in my chest and then my chest is just my chest again.

I want one of them to want something. I want to walk in and have Roxy say I need this and I cannot do it and you have to. Nobody has said that to me since I was seventeen.

I am going to write the bad thing now.

The last time I was awake, properly awake, all the way through, was the two years I spent walking with Eris before she left. Not the night. The years. Every day of it I did not know if she would still be there and every day of it I was alive.

And then the morning with the letter, and I want to say it destroyed me, and it did, but it also gave me something and I have never told anyone what. It gave me a thing to carry. I had a wound and I could take it out and look at it. For seven years I had that.

Now she is downstairs and she is mine and I have nothing to carry.

I am not saying I want her to go. I am saying I do not know what I am anymore now that nobody is leaving.

I cannot tell any of them this. What would I even say. You are all good to me and it is killing me. They would look at me. Sylphie would go quiet and think it was her, and it is not her, it has never once been her.

So I write it here and I close the book and I go down and I make breakfast, and Roxy will say something warm about it because she always does, and I will get my four seconds.

There is a way to keep the kitchen warm all night without burning anything. Sylphie's hands crack in the winter. I have most of it worked out. I have had most of it worked out since Tuesday and I have been lying up here for three nights doing the last part in my head instead of sleeping, and when I finish it she is going to put her hand on my neck.

A man of thirty four with a house and a title and five children lying in the dark solving a heating problem so someone will touch the back of his neck for four seconds.

And I want it. I want it right now, so much my hands are shaking and I am not cold.

I will have it finished by Friday.

Nothing is wrong.
