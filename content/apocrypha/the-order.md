---
title: "The Order"
created: 2026-08-07
---

Nothing is wrong.

You are at the end of the bed. Since Monday. Three nights and not one word out of you, and I know that trick, you sit and you sit and you wait for a man to get to it on his own so that when he says it, it came out of his mouth and not yours, and then it is his.

Sylphie is kind to me. She has never once been unkind to me, not when I was a child and not now. Roxy is kind to me. Eris came back and she is kind to me in her way and she has stayed and stayed and stayed.

Everything I asked for happened. All of it.

Say something. Anything. You can say the heater is a fine heater.

I fixed the heater Monday. Nobody asked me. Sylphie said thank you and put her hand on the back of my neck and that was enough, it was enough, I am telling you it was enough. Four seconds. The first time I did not count, the first time it just happened to me. The second time I counted. The third time I counted before it started.

Wednesday I was up before anybody and I did the ice off the yard, the whole yard, and I came back up and got into the bed with my boots wet on purpose so I would be lying in it when she found it, and my heart was going. Over ice. Over a yard of ice. She hit my shoulder at dinner. Four.

It is boredom. I have decided it is boredom.

Say it is not boredom. Go on. Three nights and you have got a mouth.

They never ask me for anything. Not one of them. Three people in this house who love me and not one has ever needed a thing off me, so I invent things to carry down the stairs, and they take them, and they are warm about it, every time, they are so warm about it, and I feel it in my chest, and then it goes, and then my chest is a chest.

Roxy came up Tuesday because the lamp was on. She did not need anything. She wanted to know was I coming down and I said in a minute, and she said good, and she went. And write it accurate. Not that she came. That she went. She believed me. Ask me twice. Nobody has asked me twice since I was seventeen and I am not saying that downstairs so I am saying it up here to a thing sat on my bed.

It works. All of it works, you have got the file, you know it works. And after, there is a bit where somebody has an arm over me and she is going under and she does not want one thing in the world, not one thing, and I am lying under the arm of a woman who wants nothing and I am doing sums.

I am not taking it back.

The last time I was awake, all the way through, the whole way through, was the two years walking with Eris before she went. Not the nights. The years. Every morning of it I did not know if she would be there and every morning of it I was alive, and I have tried to say that in a way that does not sound like what it sounds like and there is no way to do it, so there it is.

Then the letter.

It destroyed me. I have said that where people could hear it and I meant it, and it gave me something, and I have never told anyone what.

I do not want her to go. Write that one first, write it before anything else and write it in my hand, I do not want her to go. She is downstairs. She is mine. Five children and a title and a house that holds its heat and I would not give an hour of it back for the road.

She has stayed and stayed and stayed.

Stay. It is what you say to a dog. It is also the last word of the wedding and I do not know which one I have been saying and I have had all night on it and I have had seven years on it. When Eris says she is staying she means she is not going. That is all it means. That is the whole of the word.

I keep going at it like there is a room behind it.

There is no room behind it. She is not going.

I want her to mean something else by it and there is nothing else in it, and I have been lying up here three nights over a word that a woman used correctly.

I still have the letter.

It is under the floor on the left with the deeds and the ring. Third down. I know it is third down.

Do you see.

I have not touched it and I know what order it is in.

I read it Tuesday. Before Roxy came up. That is why the lamp was on.

Seven years I had a thing I could take out and look at. I took it out Tuesday with her asleep two rooms off and it still works. It still works. My hands went and my chest went the way they used to go and for about a minute I was back out on the road with nothing, no house, no name, no bed, and it was better.

Put that in whatever it is you keep.

I said carry. I have been saying carry all night, a thing to carry, things to carry down the stairs. You carry a body or a bucket or a debt. I have been through all three up here and it is not any of them. You carry a thing because somebody has to and there is nowhere to set it down.

I had somewhere to set it down.

It has been under that floor seven years and I go and get it.

So it is not carrying. I do not have the other word.

It stops working. Fourth time, fifth. Same letter, same words, and Tuesday I got about a minute out of it. So I went looking for something with more in it.

And I found it.

You know I found it. You were sat right there when I found it.

I worked out which one would go first.

Not would. Could.

I did it properly. I did it the way I would do a fight, and it took most of a night, and I have got the order. I know which one has got somewhere else to be. I know which one is here for the children and which one is here for the house and which one has never in her whole life been kept anywhere by anything, and that one is third, and I do her last because I am saving her.

It works better than the letter. It works every time.

That is what I do now to get to sleep. I did it Wednesday after the ice with the boots still wet on the floor. It is the one hour in the week I am not bored. Put it in the book. Put the whole hour in the book.

And I went further down the list than that.

I am not saying where I stopped.

I did not stop.

You were sat there with your hands in your lap and I got to the small ones and I did not stop, and you did not either, you did not put a hand out, you let it run, and in the morning I made the porridge.

You never put a hand out. Twenty years and not one time.

Sylphie. You pointed at Sylphie.

Roxy, you pointed at Roxy.

Eris, you said hold the line, hold it, do not move, and I held it and she came back through that door because you said she would come back through that door.

Every single thing in this house is yours.

I have never wanted one thing before you wanted it for me. I went back through it tonight, all of it, I went back to the mud, and there is nothing in there that started in me. Not the boxing. Not the house. Not one of the three of them, not one.

And I am not on the floor about it.

I am not on the floor about it, I want you to see I am not on the floor about it, I am sat up in my own bed telling you what I found because you knew what I found and I would rather be the one saying it.

So it was never that I got everything I asked for.

I have never asked for anything.

There has never been one thing.

And you knew. You knew what it would do to me. You gave me three of them and the house and the title and you knew there would be nobody left to take any of it off me, and you sat down on the end of the bed to watch, and you are still sat there, so we both know this is not the last of it.

There is a way to keep a kitchen warm all night without burning the place down. Her hands crack in the winter. Same two places on each hand, every year, and she says it is nothing. I have had most of it since Tuesday. I have been up here three nights doing the last part of it in my head instead of sleeping.

Give me the hands.

That is all I am asking for tonight, give her the winter without the hands, and you know what I wanted to put on the end of that.

She is going to put her hand on my neck.

A man of thirty four with a house and a title and five children lying in the dark solving a heating problem so that someone will touch the back of his neck for four seconds.

I want it. Right now, so much my hands are shaking and I am not cold.

I will have it finished by Friday.

Then what.

Go on then. You did not come up three nights for a heater. Give me the next one. Do not dress it, I am not going to argue, I have never once argued with you, I have never once even asked you a question, and we are well past the part where I pretend I am going to start.

I am going down to make the breakfast. Roxy will say something warm about it because she always says something warm about it. I will get my four. I will smile at her.

I will be doing the order while she says it.

Nothing is wrong.
