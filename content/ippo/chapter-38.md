Another day in the hospital, and a headache keeps striking the moment I look at anything related to X-ray images of bones. The kind of headache that captures every bit of your brain without you knowing exactly what happened. I ruled out the flu. I ruled out low glucose, just in case. The headache is still here.

It takes me back to my first rotation. The pressure I felt in my head back then makes this one feel like cake. All I can do is work.

As I move through the hospital, an image keeps flashing behind my eyes. I can't give it my attention. I turn my head away, hard, and walk toward the place with the most people. The waiting room.

At the edge of my vision, a beautiful eye catches me. My face goes red, I came in too fast, and now every eye in the room is on me. I try to look up at the ceiling and walk slowly, but it makes me feel like a clown. The image I was running from catches up.

I walk to a corner where I can see everyone without being seen. And I spot her again.

The woman with the beautiful eyes. My chest tightens higher. Something blocks my breath and keeps me staring. Her makeup is done. Everything about her is worked out. Her husband is beside her. They look like nothing could bother them, like they are perfect together.

My eyes drop to my feet. There's a spot on my shoe. I don't want to remove it. It looks beautiful just the way it is, like them, sitting next to each other, and I take another peek at the couple's file.

The more I look, the more the jealousy settles. Not for him. Not for wanting what she has in that way. It's the energy they give off, the pressure escapes my body and leaves an empty space behind. That is the problem. Them, happy next to each other.

Her face. Her hazel eyes, the mascara working with them perfectly. She has put real effort into looking this good, not today, years of it, I'm sure of it. The moment I try to do the same, I look like a mess. She doesn't.

Her hair is silky and smooth. Cut short. Just like mine. The way it drops on the sides, someone spent real money on an artist to give her that. Or maybe her husband cut it for her.

My hand goes up to my own hair. I try to feel what she has. To push my fingers between the strands, to feel the texture on my fingertips. To hum something with my hand buried in it, the way I imagine she does when she's alone.

I drag my fingers along it, slow, like a brush. Every impurity stands up. Every strand refuses. Like a cat straight out of a shower.

The same hair I walked in with.

Hers is better.

Her husband stays beside her. My heart tightens harder. I bring my hand up to it and press, trying to ease my way into the image. They are close. He leans in and whispers something into her ear, maybe because the room is loud, maybe because he needs to do something like this for her to hear him.

The hand I have pressed to my chest starts to ache in its own way. It reminds me of every time I waited alone at home for my brother, scared for him, and nothing I did to the waiting made it any smaller.

I need to go. I need to find the ER, find another patient, find something, standing here is going to split my head open. He whispers to her again and she almost laughs, almost, and that's the one I can't stand. My body moves before I decide to.

I push off the wall and walk.
