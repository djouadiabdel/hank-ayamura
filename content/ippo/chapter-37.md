The light finds my eyes before I'm ready for it.

I turn my face into the pillow and the pillow smells like me, which is the wrong smell this morning, too close, and I turn back. The ceiling is the ceiling. The desk is the desk. The belt is on the wall where the belt lives and I know where it is without looking at it and I don't look at it.

I'm fine. I slept.

I sit up and my hip tells on me.

Not pain. A warmth, low and to the side, the shape of something pressing back up through the muscle from underneath. I put my palm flat on it and push and the warmth spreads under my hand and doesn't go anywhere. I push harder. It stays. I take my hand away and the warmth is still there, quieter, waiting. I tell my hip it's fine and swing my legs over the side of the bed.

My tongue moves against my teeth and finds the taste.

Rubber and the old fish-oil softness of the guard, faint, the way a smell stays in a jacket after you've hung it up. I worked my teeth last night. I brushed. I rinsed. The taste is still in the gum line, in the small pockets behind the molars where nothing reaches. I run my tongue along the back of my teeth and it comes up stronger, the way a bruise comes up stronger when you press it.

I swallow. The taste goes down with the swallow and comes back up after.

Fine. It's just the guard. It always tastes like this the morning after.

I stand. The room stands with me. I don't look left, where the belt is. I look at the window. The curtain is moving a little. The air is the good kind of air, the kind from the port, the kind that means I could go for a run and the run would be good and my legs would be good and the morning would be the kind of morning I've had a thousand of.

I go downstairs without looking back into the room.

---

At the gym Coach is talking and I am listening the way I listen when I need to show him I'm listening.

Footwork. More footwork. He's moving his hand in the air to show me something about the angle of a step and his eyes are on me the way his eyes are on me when he needs me here, and I am here, I am watching his hand, I am watching his mouth, I am.

A bag goes off behind me. Heavy bag. The dull heavy sound of a fist going deep.

My head turns before I tell it to.

It's Kimura on the bag. Just Kimura. I turn back to Coach and Coach is still talking, he didn't stop, and I don't know if he saw me turn or if he's letting me have it. His eyes don't tell me. His eyes are on my eyes and his hand is still moving in the air.

_I could have trained more,_ I think, because thinking it is what I do now when the other thing starts. _I could have trained more. That's all it was._ I say it inside my mouth like a prayer and the rubber taste is there under the words.

Coach finishes. He puts a hand on my shoulder. The hand is warm and dry and it stays a second longer than it needs to. Then he walks off to Aoki.

I stand in the middle of the gym for a moment and the gym is around me, full, the sounds I know. Itagaki is in his corner working the pads and his face is the face he makes when he's not letting anyone in. I look past him because looking at him makes my hip warm again and I don't know why and I don't want to know why.

There's an empty spot on the floor by the far wall. One glove on it. One shoe, not a pair, one. Takamura's corner the way Takamura leaves a corner. He's at camp. He's been at camp. The spot looks the way a spot looks when someone has been gone from it for a while and the gym has decided not to touch it.

Probably sparring Miyata right now, I think.

My legs do a small thing. A twitch at the knee, the memory of a punch coming down I once didn't dodge. It passes. I wrap my arms across my chest for a second, for no reason, and then I unwrap them because there's no reason.

I go to the bag.

I shadow first. Two rounds. My body moves. My body is my body, it's doing the thing, jab jab slip, jab jab slip, I can feel the floor through my shoes and the floor is the floor. Between rounds I sit on the stool and I stretch my left leg out, the hip, just a little, just the pull I know. I've done this stretch a thousand times. I do it twice this time. Once, then again, because the first time didn't quite land and I don't know what that means.

When I look up Itagaki is looking at me.

He's maybe two meters away. His mouth is a little open. He's about to say something, his chest has taken in the breath for it, and I see the breath go in and I see him hold it.

I stand up.

I go to the heavy bag and I hit it.

One straight right, full weight, the way Coach taught me, body into it, hip through, the whole chain from the back foot up. The bag takes it. The sound comes out of the bag.

The sound is the sound. The sound the bag makes when I hit it the way I hit it. The sound I know.

I hit it again and the sound is the same.

I hit it a third time and the sound is the same and I let the sound sit in the gym for a second, in my ears, in my chest where the pressure was building, and the pressure goes down a little. The sound is the same. The sound is the same as it always was. I knew it would be. I knew, I knew.

Behind me Itagaki's breath comes out. Whatever he was going to say, he doesn't say. I hear the small scuff of his shoes turning back to his corner.

I hit the bag again. Same sound.

I'm fine. I trained more today than yesterday. That's what it was. That's all it ever was.

My hip is warm and my mouth tastes like rubber and the belt is on the wall at home where I didn't look at it and the sound of the bag is the same sound. The sound is the same. I keep hitting.
