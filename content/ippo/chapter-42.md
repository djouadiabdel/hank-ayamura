---
title: "Chapter 42"
---

I can't seem to run like the old days. That figures, after all, I am aging.

The wind smashes my face. The way it slips by my cheeks, making them look as sharp and as tight as ever. You would think that this air bending across my body is a sign that I should stop, but no. No. The air is not bending my cheeks. The air is being bent by the presence of my face.

The same air glides through my eyes, drying them from the constant pressure. And despite all of that, you don't ever see me trying to close them. No, I have to feel that pressure upon my eyes in the same manner I did with my cheeks. They are twins of the same puzzle. Even if I speed up and it hits me harder, I would not dare to close my eyes. This is the same feeling I have been chasing daily since I was eight years old.

And it can never grow old.

---

The noise of the wind changes as I come across a building that always gave me the sensation of vomiting, despite me being unaware of what is inside of it. My stomach growls back at me, this is not my pancreas feeling the run, no, not at all. I am old but gold, haha. It is my stomach, and I can feel the rice and the meat in my mouth right now, that meal I had for lunch trying to find its way out. But I can't let it get out. I am not in the mood to count my calories again. Stay inside, stay inside.

My heart grows darker the moment I think of this spot. You know exactly what I am talking about, that darkness that hits you as you pass by a place where you can feel a direct repulsion pulling you away from it, but here you are passing by and taking it all because you have to. You look at the place and you don't want to look at it again. You listen to the voice telling you not to stare, and you mind your own business every single time. I think we humans have an instinct, and this instinct is what made me score goals in the stadium when I used to be a player.

This time there is a different voice roaming across the building. It hits your ears the way you would expect a screaming ghost in a haunted house, except the ghost welcomes you with a whisper. You get it, the way your eardrums feel like they would explode. And with that voice comes the desire to control it, to stay away from it to survive. I block my ears to that voice, yet blocking it does nothing but make it sound uglier.

Uglier? Am I actually saying this echo was beautiful at first?

This idea makes me curious now, to look with my eyes at the source of this sound that has been making everything that was a habit to me change. The way I used to pass by as the sun was coming down daily and ignore this place, that is changing now, and I have to run straight to it.

Let it be a high-intensity run.

I take in a big breath and the burning sensation ruptures through every bit of tissue in my lungs. They get fuller with that air filling every corner, swallowing down the world, I call it that, making my stomach swell bigger, increasing the pressure inside of it. The air is cold. My heart is not. And that mix mingling together is what I live for. You have to believe me.

With a fast burst of motion I run at max speed toward the location, and by doing so I shut my mind. You can't let your mind think of anything while you are running. You let your instincts and your years of muscle memory take hold of you. Whenever a thought comes, swipe it away and make your mind dive deep into this sensation that you are in the middle of.

Never stop until you are there.

---

The light is the first thing that is wrong.

It is not the sun. It is not the floodlights of a stadium that pour down from above and make the grass glow white and the sweat on your arms shine like oil. This light is flat. Yellow. Artificial. It sits on the ceiling like a stain and it does not pour anywhere, it just hangs there, tired, pressing the room down instead of opening it up.

My lungs are still screaming from the sprint, and the air I am pulling into them tastes different in here. Outside, even at my worst, even doubled over at the end of a run, the air carries water and dirt and something green underneath it all, something that humans have evolved to love without knowing why. In here, the air carries sweat and dust. Old sweat. The kind that has been living in the walls and in the canvas and in the floor for years. My throat closes slightly against it, the way it would if you walked into a room where someone had been sick.

I lean against the doorframe. My calves are buzzing from the sprint, that deep vibration that takes minutes to settle after a max effort. I let my weight fall into the frame and I feel my shoulders press against wood that is not smooth, it has been worn down by other shoulders before mine, I can feel the grooves.

The floor is hard. Not grass. Not turf. Not the softness that gives under your studs and pushes back up through your ankles and tells your body _yes, this is where you belong_. This floor is rigid and flat and my running shoes feel stupid on it, like wearing gloves to eat soup. My knees feel the difference immediately. Every joint in my legs is used to the forgiveness of a pitch, and this floor offers nothing. It just takes.

The ceiling is low. That is the thing I notice next. In a stadium the sky is your ceiling, and even in the indoor arenas the roof arches high enough that you forget it is there. Here the ceiling presses down. The whole room is a closed sphere, sealed, the air recycling the same breath over and over. I can feel it, the staleness of a space that does not open. My chest tightens, not from the run anymore but from the room itself. Like being inside a fist.

A cold ripple hits my chest when I see the kid.

He is not big at all. Standing in the middle of this place, if I were next to him, he would be considered short, but I am not pure-blooded. For everyone else here he might be tall. His face and his presence are all one thing, the opposite of sweet, and yet that voice is still in my ears.

I stay at the doorframe. My breathing is settling now, slower, deeper, and with every slower breath the gym sounds get louder. The thudding. Leather against leather. Feet shuffling on that hard floor. Rope skipping somewhere to my left, the rhythm of it faster than any footwork drill I ever ran. My body does not like these sounds. They are not the sounds of play. There is no whistle. No one is calling a pass. No one is celebrating. The sounds in here are the sounds of labor, of something being hit over and over with the expectation that hitting it is the point.

My stomach turns again but I press my back harder into the doorframe.

The smell of dust thickens the deeper I breathe. I can feel it settle on my tongue, dry and chalky, nothing like the mineral smell of wet grass after a rain when the pitch is fresh and the boots sink in just slightly and the world smells like it was made for you to run on. This place smells like it was made for no one. Like it exists despite people, not because of them.

The kid stops what he is doing.

He looks like he has drifted to another world. Then he does something with his body, a movement, I don't know what to call it, and the ache I've been carrying from the run settles deep into my bones. But it feels different now. The same quiet feeling I used to borrow from my father's shadow when I was still a kid watching him train on the green grass field.

I don't understand why it feels like that. I don't want to understand it. I stretch my right calf against the doorframe, pressing my heel down into that hard floor, and the floor gives me nothing back.

---

He is sitting alone now. During training and after training, sitting alone, looking at his hands.

Is this how I look at the ball and my legs?

His eyes give me a deep ache mixed with a strange calmness. A fellow athlete, although a monster, because he is after all a boxer. My fingers grip the doorframe behind me. The wood grooves press into my fingertips. I don't let go.

He stands up. His eyes are sharp, focused on the ceiling of this place, and I wonder what he is looking for up there. There is nothing up there. Just that tired yellow light pressing down on everything.

That's interesting. I will remember that face.

The air outside hits my skin the moment I step back through the door and it is clean and wide and mine again. My lungs open like they were being held shut. The sky is above me. The ground gives slightly under my shoes. Everything is where it should be.

Well, gotta run back home. Max speed this time but for long.
