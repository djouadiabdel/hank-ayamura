The gym door shut behind him and the sound of it shutting was the last warm thing he would hear for a while.

Itagaki's foot hit the pavement and his leg screamed outward toward the dark, and the dark took him without asking. There was no direction to it. There was a shadow that had been in the window a few minutes ago and the shadow was somewhere out here and his legs had decided that was enough of a reason. His chest had decided too. His chest had been deciding things since the bell rang.

The cold found him from behind first. The gym's warmth was leaving the back of his shirt the way a hand leaves a shoulder, slow and without permission, and the cold walked into the space the warmth gave up. It found the strip of skin under his headband. It found the wet patch between his shoulder blades. It found the back of his neck and stayed there.

His skin itched. The hairs on his arms twisted upward and then twisted again, looking for somewhere to go that wasn't this. His face slapped itself red the way a face does in a cold shower you didn't choose. His lungs took a breath and the breath had a piece of metal in it, not from the distance he'd run, because he hadn't run far, but from the speed, the same speed that was supposed to be the thing he had over everyone, the thing that was supposed to mean something tonight.

His fingers found the fabric of his pants and held on. The sweat on his fingertips made the holding-on slippery and uncomfortable, and he let go.

He had stopped running a while ago. He hadn't noticed.

The sidewalk under his foot was harder than the gym floor. There was no give in it. There was nothing under him that bounced back the way the canvas bounced back, the way the mat under the heavy bag bounced back, and the absence of that bounce made his legs feel like they were standing on someone else's body.

A heel struck the sidewalk somewhere on his right and the sound went straight into his ear like a needle. His hand went up to cover the ear without his permission and on the way up it caught the edge of the headband and dragged it loose.

The headband slid down. It sat low on his forehead now, half over his right eye, sitting at an angle that wasn't his.

His fingers went to fix it.

His fingers found the elastic in the wrong place. They found it sitting at the angle of a head that had turned to look at something it shouldn't have been looking at. They found the angle of Ippo's head in the second before the bell, the small turn, not toward the opponent, not toward the corner, toward the window. Toward the shape in the window. The angle was barely an angle. It was the kind of turn you only see if you're watching a person the way Itagaki had been watching him.

His fingers stayed on the elastic. The angle stayed under his fingers.

He pulled the headband off his head.

It came away wet. The elastic in his palm was the weight of a squeezed towel and a few cold drops slid down the back of his neck and kept going under his shirt, and the cold under his shirt found new places to be cold in, and his shoulders pulled in toward each other without asking him.

His hand closed around the headband. It closed harder than he meant it to. The small cuts the rope had left on his palm earlier, when he'd been gripping the ropes from outside the ring, watching, gripping harder than a person watching a fight should grip anything, the small cuts opened a little under the pressure and the blood found the wet of the fabric and the two became one color in his hand.

He squeezed, then loosened, then squeezed. The rhythm was something his hand was doing on its own. It was the rhythm of a person trying to think.

Maybe Ippo had been tired. Coach had been working him hard. Maybe it was the recovery, maybe his body wasn't all the way back. Maybe the window thing had been the kind of feint Aoki was always trying to pull, eyes drifting somewhere to throw the other guy off, just badly timed. Maybe.

The maybe arrived in his chest before it arrived in his head and his chest unclenched a small amount, and the unclenching felt good, and the good feeling came too fast, and somewhere underneath the good feeling something else was watching the good feeling arrive and not believing it.

His hand started to lift the headband back toward his head.

He was almost smiling. The voices from inside the gym were coming back to him through the door. Takamura's voice, loud about something, the regular noise of the regular night, and his hand was lifting the headband and his face was almost ready to put the regular night back on.

A footstep landed on the other side of the street.

It wasn't a heel this time. It was a softer step, and it had stopped. Whoever had taken the step had taken one and then decided not to take another, and the not-taking was what Itagaki's body heard.

His eyes moved before his hand did. His hand stayed where it was, the headband halfway between his palm and his head, the elastic still wet, the blood and the water still mixing.

There was a shape across the street under the place where the streetlight didn't quite reach. The shape was standing very still. The kind of still that a person has to practice. The kind of still Itagaki had only seen on one other person, in one other place, in a photograph on the wall above the lockers, the one nobody talked about, the one with the cleaner gloves and the cleaner stance and the name underneath it that the gym used like a weather report.

His fingers forgot the headband.

The shape across the street did not move.
