title: "Chapter 41"
---

As I enter the gym the same air that hit me yesterday when I punched Takamura rushes onto my chest. The air gathers in that same spot where I had been standing, where Takamura's side stayed unreactive after my punch landed.

The thought of it melts something in my soul into a delighted sensation that fills every part of me. I try not to jump where I stand but the feeling is too strong. It's the same clean pull I used to chase every time I left Tokyo for a fight, the pull that used to fix everything.

My father calls me to put on my gloves. The sensation of the gloves before I wear them makes those emotions grow heavier. The leather feeling on the tips of my fingers. The weight of them in my hands — the same hands that will carry that weight onto my opponent.

I notice my bare hands and realize I forgot to wrap them before putting on the gloves. I look left and right, wondering if anyone has seen something this silly. If anyone noticed, their eyes would turn down the volume on the delight I carried in.

The air still carries that yesterday’s punch, the one that landed clean while Takamura’s side stayed completely still. My lungs fill with it again, the same way they did when I first stepped into the ring years ago. My shoulders lift on their own, the same clean pull I used to chase every time I left Tokyo for a fight and tried to fix everything. My feet stay planted, but my chest lifts on its own. The delight spreads deeper now, warming the space between my ribs like something I’ve been waiting to feel again for a long time.

But I don't know why I'm pulled straight back to the old pull I borrowed from my father's craft.

I shake my head and take down the tape to wrap my hands.

The pressure on each finger rises constantly as I thread the tape. I’ve done this thousands of times — I could do it without eyes. But the warmth of my bare skin against the glove leather makes the wrongness physical, not just forgotten but felt. My fingers splay inside the gloves trying to feel for the tape that should be there, searching for what belongs between skin and leather. The tape holds down my blood in a continuous thread of movement. In the middle of that pressure I feel something like what must come during a ring, though I’ve never felt this exact thing before. To feel pressure first is the first step to being free. These are the same echoes I used to feel before fights that meant something to my father. My wrists turn on their own, tightening the wrap until the familiar squeeze travels up my forearms and settles into the same old place it always did when I was preparing to step in front of him.

The pressure holds down my blood in a continuous thread of movement. In the middle of that pressure I feel something like what must come during a ring, though I've never felt this exact thing before. To feel pressure first is the first step to being free. These are the same echoes I used to feel before fights that meant something to my father.

When I'm ready with my gloves on, my father puts my hands on his chest and throws one slow punch into me. It looks slow and weak but the vibration travels through my ribs without waiting. My ribs can't absorb it — the vibration moves down my spine into the floor. I try to balance myself against being shattered by it but I hardly move from my spot. This is like the same punch I landed on Takamura when he threw a jab toward me and I used his weight against him.

This is the same strength I used to borrow from Takamura's shadow every time I watched him train, waiting for the day I could take him down. But that pressure is strange.

The pressure is strange because it doesn’t stop at the shoulder the way mine always has. It keeps going down, through the hips, through the legs, all the way into the floor. My father’s punch didn’t just move the bag — it moved the ground itself. I feel the vibration still humming under my soles, the same full-body click I used to feel when I watched champions who never seemed to miss the floor. My stance widens on its own, heavier now, as if the floor is pulling me down and pushing me up at the same time.

I pull away from my father toward the bags to throw punches.

Hundreds of the same punch as my father corrects the same hip-shoulder line over and over again.

My hands start to hurt differently — a deep ache of mass transfer instead of sharp speed pain. My father keeps correcting my technique, which means I have to keep throwing. The ache can't rest. No matter how I try to clench my fist harder to make it hurt less, this turns the volume up on the pain, killing any joy I had from entering the gym.

The pain pushes me to endure. My father keeps correcting, so it must be the technique. I keep trying to follow his advice but the sharpness won't escape my fist. My fists clench automatically, trying to squeeze the hurt out, but it only makes the ache spread deeper. This is the same deep ache I used to feel when I tried to match Takamura's power, when my body saw his and tried to copy it.

This memory makes me want to remember Takamura's punches. How does he do it?

For the first time I feel the ground travel through my body into the bag, not the other way around. The whole-body vibration humming under my feet. Something clicking in my spine. All of it traveling with speed into my punch. When it lands, that heaviness in my hand becomes the same full-body click. It lands exactly like those old fight tapes of champions who never seemed to miss the floor. Like Takamura.

The heaviness stays in my hand long after the bag stops swinging. My knuckles feel thicker, my wrist locked in a way it never was before. The same deep ache I used to feel when I tried to match Takamura’s power in the gym spreads up my forearms and settles into my shoulders. My legs feel heavier than they should, the floor still humming under my feet like it’s waiting for the next punch. I can feel the ground inside me now, not just under me.

As I think of how Takamura does it, he notices what I just did and sees me looking at him. With his bare hand, he throws that same punch into his bag. His bag makes a beautiful, murderous sound. With low effort the bag swings back far, as if a bomb hit it. Then he stares at me in a weird way.

Weird Takamura.

I stand there, hands still buzzing from what just happened. The ground is still there under my feet, waiting. I can feel it humming.
