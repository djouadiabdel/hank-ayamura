My body shakes like wobbling pudding. The gym's darkness pushes in from outside, but artificial light cuts it back, harsh fluorescence that makes everyone's stares feel sharper. Their eyes try to burrow under my skin. My skin is thick, I tell myself. Over and over. But my attention divides the way it always does near a cut I've taken too far, except this time I'm not even bleeding yet.

Takamura's spears ravage through my body as I try to find an opening, moving forward and sideways to keep the right distance. My legs won't let me do anything, like standing in the same ring as Ricardo Martinez.

This makes perfect sense. Takamura isn't Ricardo, but he's a world champion with the same sharpness, only heavier. No shame in shivering at what I'm looking at.

Even when I try to calm my breathing, each flinch drives me insane with thoughts of his hands piercing through me from long range. I've seen exactly what he can do. No shame in admitting that.

The real shame is thinking you can take a champion without doing anything unique. But isn't that what I'm trying to do? I'm not here as some make-a-wish kid hoping to take down a world champion without training. What am I doing in the middle of this ring, staring at this monster, if not to take him down?

Is this what my father taught me? I've been learning to fight bigger guys, just like he did over the years. Yes, he lost battles and retired, but I'm not like him. To take down someone like Takamura, or someone mightier, I need the exact weapon that can defeat anyone. Unlike my father.

Takamura steps forward, head straight, eyes that refuse to blink. You can see his eye muscles contracting, forcing themselves to stay open. This makes his eyes drier than usual. I've never seen Takamura do this, usually he blinks carelessly, walking down opponents regardless.

His eyes tell the story of someone who knows what to do when taking a punch to the face. More than that. Takamura is always the one in the middle of big stages, the biggest tournaments. Taking a punch is never his concern, only winning. Even in sparring.

Maybe he doesn't see this as serious at all. Just warmup for real training. After all, Takamura is hunting another world champion, collecting more belts.

Maybe he expects my punch to feel light as a feather. The weight difference makes it clear I can't take his punch, but he can take mine. If I eat one punch from him at full strength, I might die. But my punch? It has to be calculated, like my father taught me. Turn all that rotational force back on him with a swift cross. Only then will it truly hurt.

My mind boggles as I stand, not really standing still, trying to move around the ring to avoid his jabs. But I'm certain he'll see whatever I do.

His jabs push straight through the air around me, air filled with artificial light that lets us see. Small particles float in the dusty beams, moving slowly until one of his punches passes by, ruining that steadiness, rendering it all into chaos.

Relax. Relax. Remember father's lesson. I have to spot the exact position for the correct punch. I have to not die.

My eyes track his legs moving in patterns I've seen from fighters like him. The heaviness is apparent, you try to move forward but risk stepping wrong, giving him the chance to use all that weight and pressure back at you.

But his rhythm is delicate and efficient, losing no power. The slowness I expected from his weight isn't there because of the technique he's using. Professional level. A world of difference.

My lips bite hard on the mouthguard as I try to calm down, not think of that body punch I took, the one that reminds me of Ippo on the canvas making that drum sound, looking shocked.

I expect to be punched eventually, but that constant image makes me avoid that line of thinking. I look left and right, nothing will ease my mind enough to focus. I could call off the fight, but that means I'm a quitter. I'll never accept being one.

The thought of quitting makes my blood boil, clouds my thinking about the next move. Searching for an opening, I see none as Takamura keeps straight-punching the air.

My only rescue is to walk in and out, hunt for that voice or light, bait out a punch like I always do. Hunt for that cross.

His eyes blink quickly now, getting disturbed. They don't look like they're working well, maybe that extra focus he put in earlier. He takes milliseconds to adjust to whatever's on his right side.

I step in, using the rotational pull to take that punch and turn it back at him, even though my heart drops with every centimeter forward. Like standing next to a weapon.

Takamura moves exactly like a shadow I felt earlier in the session, beaming with light telling me to take the road forward. I try to look at the light and it never leaves its spot, exactly as Takamura repeats a certain movement to his right side.

I see a light, a road I can take. Might be a trap, he resets his stance because he's already closed it down. But wait, am I seeing something wrong? Even after resetting to push me forward across the ring, he's still throwing it the same way. Over and over. The same answer.

The ground feels farther away from me.

He throws a punch. I step across with everything my father taught me, rotational force meeting rotational force, and something wrong happens to his right eye, a flinch, a moment where his focus scatters like those dust particles in the light.

What?

The heaviest hands in boxing don't feel heavy anymore as I push them toward his body. The sharpest eyes in boxing aren't looking where they should be.

He's standing between two pillars and both have hairline cracks. His new weight has nowhere to aim.

Takamura loud, crude, swaggering. "Finally learned to hit, ice princess?", but the performance is so complete I almost believe I imagined the rest.

Miyata alone.

The decision to call Ippo sits in my hand like a phone I haven't picked up yet.
