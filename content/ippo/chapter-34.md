---
title: "Chapter 34"
---

Miyata slid into the car, the old leather seat hitting his back and spreading him across the whole seat, shoulder blades dropping. His head found the headrest and the pressure that had been holding it up cooled the way it always cooled after he made weight, slow, leaking out from the base of the skull. The leather smell turned the tip of his nose slightly red, the kind of red you see on a sick man. He pressed his nose left and right in a snap motion, but it was dry. No mucus. Just the smell.

His body kept spreading into the seat until it reached something it couldn't spread past. He pushed. The something pushed back, an immovable wall, countering him, shoving him back into his corner. He pushed again and got pushed further. Eyes still forward, he dropped his gaze to his own lap and saw a meaty leg on top of his thigh.

His hand gathered its strength and tried to move it. No use. His palm met a soft, uncontracted sphere of muscle, the softness of a relaxed man's thigh, the kind you only feel by accident. His heart skipped, then rewound, refusing to go forward. He squeezed a little harder. The squeeze made his heart crumble deeper, into the corner of a man hunted by a wild boar. A spear ripped through his face and eyes. His hand clenched tighter on the thigh and his body dropped into fight-or-flight. His eyes glimpsed the man beside him.

His heart fell into the seat.

The clenching stopped at once. Takamura. The same serious face he wore against the American world champion he wanted dead.

Miyata pulled himself into his corner, giving Takamura more room than he needed. Takamura stretched back into his laid-down posture and started singing, random songs that killed every last bit of the weight-cut high Miyata had left. Then, between two lines of the song, it came: the punch landing on Ippo's face. The way the fat and the muscle had absorbed the hit, expanding, transferring the damage across the skull in that consecutive fashion Miyata only knew from the ring, from his own counters.

His stomach growled. His hand squeezed itself out of its uncomfortable spot and went to hold the stomach down, but the voice of his stomach only got louder. Takamura turned with a lazy face, half a look at Miyata, half at the hand on the stomach.

"Miyata, you hungry? Don't worry about it, I'll get some fish for you."

Miyata squinted half his face at him, _as if you could._ Takamura's one look back made Miyata want to sweat out of his clothes. He glanced at the window. Closed. His life flashed in front of him.

"Okay, okay. I trust you, Takamura."

Takamura kept singing. The usual one.

_I am strong of strength, I am king of kings,_ _Miyata will notice my greatness in and out of the ring._

Over and over. Miyata's pressured-up body wanted to throw itself out of the car. His father, soft and curious from the front, asked Takamura if he was ready. Takamura laughed out loud.

"You think I'm that little kid Ippo, not to be ready?"

He kept laughing, half a look at Miyata. Miyata's eyes dropped to his lap. He wiped the emotion out of them and centered them on one spot. He tried to lay his head in his palm to close at least one ear, but Takamura kept pushing him, a shove here, a shove there, at random, until Miyata's cheek scratched against the last tape mark on his left hand.

The tape mark from the last training session. The session where he'd been watching the clock, waiting to finally see what Takamura had for him. He settled himself on scratching off that last spot. Ideas and thoughts rose as he scratched, and the more they rose, the harder he tried to focus on every small dot of tape until there was none left, thinking that it had been like Ippo's drumming voice, the way it slammed the floor and left no room for Ippo to be Ippo.

His jaw clenched at the frame being drawn in his mind. The jaws wrapped so tightly around each other that nothing could pass between them. The lines of the cut, the weight-cut sharpness, stood out on his face, and underneath them a burning sensation started to climb the more he held the clench. He found a clue in it. Something to focus on. The burn echoed across his jaw and face, tingling every nerve, and with each cold breath the echo turned warm as it passed through his throat into his lungs.

The singing stopped.

Miyata didn't notice it stop. He noticed the absence of it, the car suddenly being just the car, tires on road, his father's breathing in the front, the faint tick of the indicator. The warmth in his throat kept climbing in the quiet, louder now that nothing was covering it.

Takamura's voice came back low. Almost a murmur, aimed at the front seat.

"I've been taking more hits lately."

A beat. The tires.

"Part of the plan, even."

Another beat. Miyata's father didn't answer right away.

"Well, not really part of the plan. But the fact that I can take them from weaklings means something's getting cooked up in there."

The quiet held for one more second. Then Takamura was back to the loud singing as if he'd never dropped out of it at all.

Miyata's father smiled a little. Miyata's jaw stopped clenching. It dropped fully, with no support holding it down, free-floating on whatever tendon kept it attached to his body...

"Am I not right, little underlings?"

Miyata nodded while still stretching his mouth. His eyes stopped on his chest. Downward, intact in their place, despite the laughing and the screaming Takamura was still doing. For a split second he was going to stop nodding, but the car hit a speed bump, lifting him up and forcing the nod the rest of the way through.

The car slowed. Takamura was nodding at something Miyata's father said now, something about work, something about the road, and Miyata was watching Takamura's face without hearing the words. In his chest a door that used to be bolted was swinging. Not open. Not closed. Just swinging, back and forth, the bolt sitting on the floor beside it where he couldn't reach.

It swung while Takamura was still mid-sentence. It swung between Takamura's face and Miyata's chest, in the gap where the nodding should have meant agreement.

The car pulled into the camp. His father said something about work, the same sentence he always said when he dropped Miyata anywhere, the one that meant _I won't see you for a while._ Miyata got out. He lifted his bag. His hands, when they closed around the strap, felt like they belonged to someone else. Someone whose assumption had cracked and who hadn't yet decided what to do about it.

He shouldered the bag and started walking toward the camp entrance. Behind him, Takamura was already singing again.
