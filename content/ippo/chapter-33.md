title: "Chapter 33"
---

Miyata's figure across the street made Itagaki stare at him from far, and the late-night sky dimness made focusing on a moving object burn in the back of his eyes. The streetlight on the sides ate the edges of his vision into a silhouette.

He narrowed his eyes into a squint to push the shape back into focus. The squint held for a second and then the burn climbed deeper, so he wiped the wet from the corner with his fist. The wipe smeared the streetlight into a streak across his vision and Miyata blurred worse than before. When the streak cleared his foot was already over the curb.

His heart dropped as his foot almost landed. The highway that always delivers the side-air splashes from cars passing by at night with their dimmed lights, and his legs froze on the spot, the same freeze his body does in the ring when he makes a wrong movement and a punch is already on its way.

He opened his eyes. Miyata had turned away from the whole conversation and was walking off. Itagaki noticed the width of his back under the semi-classical suit and tried to call him back, but all that left his mouth was a half-broken word, the kind a child uses to notify you he stopped. His face turned red.

Miyata's back was not as big as Ippo's. Ippo's back is never hidden in terms of thickness no matter what he is wearing. And here he was, feeling the chill down his face from the whole incident, holding his cheek with his hand the same way he did the time he watched Miyata on the ring punching that bloody cross into an opponent.

He glared his eyes again at Miyata's back and noticed the distance between them. A few seconds had already been enough for Miyata to glide through a long stretch of sidewalk without breaking a sweat. That bloody cross got him a KO, Itagaki thought. He uses the opponent's whole body against him.

Then he thought: Miyata can't do that against Senpai.

The moment he uttered it to himself, Miyata stopped walking again.

Itagaki's eyes opened and closed in a twitching rhythm, and in a thin, wind-like voice he said that Ippo was not in his best condition, that if he had been, this wouldn't have happened. The sentence left his mouth too fast for him to catch what he had said.

The headband was still in his hand as he spoke. His fingers tightened on the elastic, and this time it was not wet anymore, it ripped through the already hurt palm and the pain made him close one eye a little the way one does trying to focus. He lifted his hand to check on it and his gaze turned away from Miyata for a second.

In an instant his body jolted like a small lightning had passed through him, and he looked up again to Miyata's glaring face and the words that dropped out of his mouth:

_Is it that you don't want to admit that your rival is strong?_

Itagaki's heart skipped a beat and then trembled into a heavy pounding. He did not know what to say, so he kept giving an ugly serious look at Miyata, but the darkness all over Miyata was glaring back at him the same way it did in high school in the ring when a title was inches away from him, that was what the sight of him now reminded him of.

Miyata said that Takamura had told him Itagaki had been chasing after Ippo since high school, and that dropped like a heavy vehicle on his back. He tried to look down with a less firm look, but it made him feel naked, alone in a way the ring never let him be, no corner man here to lift him up and tell him what to do.

He opened his mouth and explained it was not like that at all, thinking of Takamura's voice cutting in in a light beep telling Miyata that thing. The image of Imai lifting title after title from a distance flashed through him while his heart kept pounding, and Miyata cut him off, treating him like a kid:

_You should be very happy. The stronger your opening, the more fun you'll have._

A profound stillness settled over Itagaki's features. He wasn't looking at Miyata to calculate a counter. He was drinking him in, his gaze fixed on his Senpai's rival, and it signaled a brief emotional escape into a happy memory.

A face that reminded him of a moment where he could be happy for a second. Where he could train harder and put all his might into the process of getting better. Where the mistakes he makes would all be lifted away in a heartbeat, because he is training to face a strong person who will lift him up.

---

Takamura yelled at Miyata from the gym door to get to the car.

The yell hit Miyata in the lungs. For a second he stopped breathing, as if the yell had convinced him he was still inside the warm gym air, and he forced his lungs to pull more in but only coughed the dry catching-a-cold cough Itagaki knew from his own winters. Miyata turned his head toward the gym, then back, and walked away.

Itagaki stayed in the spot.

The headband was still in his hand. His legs were still pointed the way they had been when his foot almost hit the street. The conversation was leaving him, walking off down the sidewalk in Miyata's back, and it was not taking him with it. Nobody had insulted him. That was the part his chest caught on. Miyata had not raised his voice, had not pushed him, had not even looked back. The conversation had simply closed its door on his face without slamming it, and his body did not know what posture to hold for a thing that wasn't a hit.

His fingers were still clenched on the elastic.

Somewhere down the block, behind a parked car, he heard a laugh, loud, loose, the laugh of a coach who has already won something in his head. _Hahaha, when you become rookie king you've gotta challenge him, we've got nothing to fear from Ippo._ Imai's coach. The laugh carried on the cold the way sound does late at night, clean and too close.

Then Imai's voice, quieter, flat:

_I doubt the result would be the same._

The flatness landed in the same spot on his chest where Miyata's flatness had landed a minute ago. The same temperature. The same refusal to perform certainty for him. Two men in one night had said his Senpai's name in the same tired tone, and neither of them had been speaking to him. He had been a body standing on a sidewalk, hearing himself dismissed by name in a sentence that wasn't even about him.

The cold found a new place under his shirt, slid along a line he hadn't felt the cold touch before, the hollow at the bottom of his sternum where the breath has to turn around and come back.

He lifted the headband.

The motion was too fast. His wrist snapped up and the fabric came down on his forehead with a small violent slap, and this time the angle was right. The elastic sat where it was supposed to sit. The wrongness that had opened the other night, the headband over the eye, sitting wrong, was corrected.

He said it out loud, and his voice was not a wind anymore:

_I won't let you become rookie king easily._

The headband sat right. The angle was correct. But the correction felt like something else than belief sitting back where it belonged. It sat the way a helmet sits. The way armor sits. His hand stayed at his temple a second longer, pressing, making sure, and in that pressing there was a small part of him that knew he had just put the band on the wrong way, even though the angle was right.

He lowered the hand.

The street stayed where it was. The sidewalk stayed where it was. Down the block the coach was still laughing about something else now. Itagaki stood alone with the first sentence of a vow in his mouth, and the vow felt like strength, and he did not yet have a way of knowing it was the first sentence of a future collapse.
