---
title: "Chapter 35"
---

The gym corners stare at me and I can't do anything about it. I can only sit with the feeling inside me. The pressure that isn't hugging me but making me experience my own existence in the same fashion I always do when I am on a cut, trying to make weight.

I look at my hands. I push them up across my face to wash away every bit of tear that might come out of my eyes. I don't want anyone to see me crying. Especially not Takamura. Especially not my father.

I don't know what I can be. I don't know what the future will look like for me. It is like staring at a black canvas, waiting for the artist to draw something on it, and staring again at something completely black. Not a video that stopped moving forward. A still picture that has already ended its duty.

I can't keep doing this to myself.

I look at the tape on my hand. I feel the tight pressure around my finger and I start trying to pull it tighter than it already is, because I get the sense that something is wrong about it. I can't change it. I keep looking at it and the feeling never vanishes — only sharpens, as the screams of Takamura's punches hitting the bags grow harder, as I feel that my sentence has already been written.

My eyes are weary from the long travel. My head is heavy with sorrow over what is coming. I am used to thinking about the future. Not like this. Not at this intensity.

Not about Takamura. Not about any of it. But something is etching itself inside my chest.

The strength that lived in me all week is fading. My head searches for somewhere to lay itself down. My forehead beams with a warm heat that pulls my thoughts left and right. I can't look straight. My arms are moving without me. The conscious part of me is gone.

There is a ring in my hand. The same ring that reminds me of who I am as an athlete. The same ring that reminds me of the slow fall I am taking toward a world I know nothing about.

There was a time in my life when I looked at everything around me and it made perfect sense. Now I worry about the worms in the field. About the food I will put in my mouth tomorrow. About what I am even doing when I walk to the window and look out.

I find myself facing a giant.

The traps standing still on their own, moving the way a dinosaur's skin moves — armored, prehistoric, not quite human. The deltoids round and perfectly fit into the whole image of Takamura. His eyes staring back at me, trying to crush away the pressure all over me. And I am in no mood for any of it.

---

The punches land and I feel pressure spread through my hand. Sweat parts from the spot I hit, splashing off his guard. The air around it grows more fragrant with the smell. The heat melts into the sweat and the gym takes on the title it always stereotypically has. I clench my teeth at the impact, expecting a payback on my face. The light breeze of his return never comes.

His guard is calling me with open light sources across his body. One above his left eye, crying out for someone to take that path. Another at the liver, where his right hand passes mindlessly, guarding nothing. I take a step forward on my footwork. I jump here and there to give end and meet to one of the echoes.

His stance shifts a quarter inch backward. All the lights vanish from the spot. I look exactly at that frame and I swallow hard where I stand.

My eyes register every move Takamura makes. I am caught between actions, not words. To move forward or step back. The lights grow harder in the corner of the ring where Takamura is standing — the same spot I always make people stand in before I hit them with a punch that takes them straight to sleep.

My brain bulges with light and electricity as I think of punching him. To bring my hand into the same space and sphere as Takamura. No — not even that. To bring my whole body inside. To pursue the area in the desire to close every space he could escape from, until I am staring at him and he has nowhere to go.

I take a step forward. He takes one back.

The synchronized stepping on the mat drops a musical theme on the spot. You could close your eyes and understand exactly what is happening. You could smell the air and tell, by the slight differences in sweat, which fighter is on which side of the ring.

The breeze hits me straight in the face after I make him take a step back, and another. I inhale fully and embrace every bit of the coldness, every chill it offers. Like someone who has been inside a boiling sauna and finally drinks cold water and gets a breath of fresh air. It makes me picture Ippo on the ground, in the same exact way.

A butterfly feeling spreads its wings inside my stomach. It comes from the same place a good memory comes from — the kind you pull back to touch and feel calm. But this time it isn't a memory. It is a reality about to take place.

In a sudden movement, Takamura shifts his stance and his whole body proportion changes. He takes the initiative. He remodels every emotion I had about the air and the butterflies and lets me know there was a fire burning slowly next to them the whole time. The kind of fire you think will only keep you warm. The kind that ends up burning the entire camp and every tree around it.

All the courage I had mustered is crumbling to the ground. His eyes are heading toward me in an instant. My brain jots down ideas, trying to understand what just happened. The only conclusion it reaches: this isn't real life. He has teleported completely from the corner to the front of my face.

The speed of him is insane. How could someone with this much weight, this much meat on his body, travel this fast inside my field of view, inside my area of defense, after always showing me so many weaknesses I could grab and use to drop him to the ground?

My guard blocks the punch. The sound of it spreads like a bomb launched next to me. The little drops of sweat on my body collapse into the air and fall after the heat. I try to keep guarding, thinking it would be a good idea to stand still. Another punch hits me. I am sent back into the same corner he was just standing in — only now I am on the opposite side of it.

The floor feels closer to me than ever.

The fabric on the ground, the softness it offers against my skin — it feels like part of my own existence. The same existence that was on this floor during my spar with Ippo. I don't know why I am looking at it like this. I don't know why I am staring at Ippo while I feel the floor this close to me.

But what I do know is that Takamura's attack will never be the only attack I am going to take.

My lips meet the dirty, moist, earthy taste rising to them.

What can I do?
